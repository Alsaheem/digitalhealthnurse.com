If you have spent years in clinical practice, it is easy to assume digital health roles are reserved for people with coding skills, product titles or years in technology companies.

They are not.

Many digital health teams actively look for people who understand how care is delivered, how clinicians work and where systems create friction for patients and staff. That knowledge is hard to teach. It is something you already have.

![Healthcare professional reviewing digital tools](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1576091160399-112ba8d25d1d?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1600&q=80)

## What “digital health” often means in practice

Digital health is a broad umbrella. It can include:

- Supporting the adoption of clinical systems
- Helping organisations implement new tools
- Translating clinical needs for product and engineering teams
- Improving workflows so technology actually supports care

You do not need to understand every role on day one. You do need a clearer sense of which directions are realistic for *your* experience.

## A practical place to begin

Start by writing down:

1. The parts of your current role you do well
2. The problems you repeatedly solve for patients or colleagues
3. The tools and systems you already use

Those notes often reveal transferable strengths more clearly than browsing job boards alone.
